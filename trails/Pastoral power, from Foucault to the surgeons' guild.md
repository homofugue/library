Foucault's late essay names a form of power that is neither sovereign nor legal: it looks after each individual, knows their conscience, and sacrifices itself for the flock. This walk follows that form out of the church and into the places where it still works — the therapist's chair, the anatomy theatre — and ends on the one painting that shows the shepherd with a knife.

1. [[The Subject and Power]] — Start with the essay itself: 1982, Critical Inquiry, the pages you highlighted.
2. [[Foucault — organized itself as a church]] — The claim that Christianity alone organised itself as a church, and so as a pastorate.
3. [[Foucault — a knowledge of the conscience]] — The pastor must know the inside of the mind — the move that makes confession, and later the case history, possible.
4. [[Foucault — salvation in this world]] — Salvation becomes health, well-being, security: the pastorate goes secular without changing shape.
5. [[Pastoral power]] — The theme as hub: everything in the library that touches this.
6. [[The clinic]] — Where the pastorate landed. The consulting room is a confessional with a couch.
7. [[Searles — responding to other persons' unconscious processes]] — Searles's patients introject the therapist: the shepherd's conscience leaks into the flock.
8. [[Rembrandt — The Anatomy Lesson of Dr Deijman (surviving fragment)]] — Deijman opens the brain of a hanged man while the guild watches. Care, knowledge and the knife in one image.
9. [[Surgery]] — End on the theme — the cut as the pastorate's most literal form.
